The harbour wakes before the town does. The boats go out in the grey
light, and the gulls follow the boats, and the town follows the gulls
with its ears if not its eyes. By the time the bakery opens the boats
are small on the water, and the water is the colour of the sky.

Nobody in the town counts the boats, but everybody knows how many went
out. When the wind turns in the afternoon, the town turns with it: the
windows on the harbour side fill with faces, and the faces count.

The boats come back one by one. The last one is always the slowest,
and the town always waits for it.
