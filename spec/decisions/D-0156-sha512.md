# D-0156 — SHA-512 and SHA-384 in `std::crypto`

Status: ACCEPTED (2026-10-04, the owner: "proceed with the entire roadmap using your discretion", on the crypto roadmap agreed toward a DNS-over-TLS client)
Kind: Decision (`spec/02-schema.md`)
Governed by: `CobaltC_Master_Instructions.md` §1, §9 (the std helper admission test)
Depends on: D-0151 (`std::crypto`, `Sha256`)
Affects: `spec/21` §0, §2m (4.21.0), `spec/conformance.md` (3.146.0), the guide §21, `impl/std/crypto.cb`; `CHG-0184`

## Problem

Certificates on the public web are signed with ECDSA P-384 over SHA-384
and with RSA over SHA-384 and SHA-512, and TLS's AES-256 cipher suites
hash with SHA-384. The DNS-over-TLS client cannot check such a chain
with SHA-256 alone.

## Candidate mechanisms

1. **SHA-512 written in CobaltC**, as SHA-256 is (FIPS 180-4, 64-bit
   words, 80 rounds), with SHA-384 as SHA-512 from other initial values
   cut to 48 bytes. Selected.
2. **Only SHA-384**, since TLS and X.509 need it most. SHA-512 costs no
   more code and RSA-SHA-512 certificates exist. Rejected.

## Selected design

`spec/21` §2m `[Sha512]`: `Sha512` and `Sha384` as plain structs with
`new`, `update` and `finish` (64 and 48 bytes), and one-shot `sha512`
and `sha384`, mirroring `Sha256`. Constants computed from their
definitions (cube and square roots of the first primes) and checked
against FIPS's first values. Verified by FIPS 180-4's vectors as a case
and by the hash sweep (`stress/crypto/sweep.py`) against Python's
`hashlib` under all three tools.

Not adopted: HMAC-SHA-384/512 and HKDF-SHA-384 until a cipher suite that
needs them is offered (the client offers ChaCha20-Poly1305, which uses
SHA-256); SHA-512/256 and SHA-3.

## Compatibility impact

Additive: four exported items in `std::crypto`.

## Revisit conditions

- HMAC-SHA-384 and HKDF-SHA-384 with an AES-256-GCM suite.
